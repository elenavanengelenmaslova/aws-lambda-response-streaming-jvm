# Code of Conduct

This project is a small, single-maintainer example repository. The rules below are adapted from the
[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/)
(see the [Contributor Covenant homepage](https://www.contributor-covenant.org)); the wording here is
our own, the substance is theirs.

## Our pledge

Everyone taking part in this project is treated as a person first and a contributor second.
Participation is open regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic
status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

## Expected behaviour

- Show empathy and basic kindness towards other people.
- Respect differing opinions, viewpoints, and levels of experience.
- Give and accept constructive feedback gracefully. Review comments are about code, not about people.
- Take responsibility for mistakes, apologise to those affected, and learn from them.
- Weigh what is best for the wider community of readers and users, not only what is convenient for
  you.
- Keep discussion on topic. This repository exists to demonstrate JVM Lambda response streaming.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing someone else's private information, such as a physical or email address, without their
  explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or pull requests.
- Deliberately filing reports in bad faith, or retaliating against someone who filed one.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, code review comments,
commit messages, discussions, and the repository's documentation. It also applies in public spaces
when an individual is representing the project — for example posting from an official account, or
speaking as a maintainer or contributor of this project at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement contact

There is exactly one enforcement contact: the maintainer,
[@elenavanengelenmaslova](https://github.com/elenavanengelenmaslova).

Report a concern privately through GitHub. Use this repository's private reporting form — **Security
tab → Advisories → Report a vulnerability** — which reaches only the maintainer. The form is
labelled for vulnerabilities; state in the first line that your report concerns the Code of Conduct
rather than a security issue. Do not report conduct concerns through public issues or pull requests.

Two notes on this route:

- GitHub private vulnerability reporting must be enabled in the repository settings for the form to
  appear. If the **Report a vulnerability** button is not there, the setting has not been switched on
  yet and there is no private channel; wait for it rather than filing the report publicly.
- A maintainer may substitute a direct email address for this route later. If that happens, this
  section is the place it changes, and `SECURITY.md` changes with it.

## What happens after a report

- **Acknowledgement within 5 business days** of the report — the same window `SECURITY.md` commits
  to.
- A status update at least every 10 business days until the matter is closed.
- The reporter's identity and the details of the report are kept confidential.

The maintainer decides what happens next, in proportion to the behaviour. The usual ladder is a
private warning, then a temporary block from interacting with the project, then a permanent ban.
Moderating content — editing, hiding, or deleting comments, commits, or other contributions that
breach this document — is available at any step, with a short explanation of why.
